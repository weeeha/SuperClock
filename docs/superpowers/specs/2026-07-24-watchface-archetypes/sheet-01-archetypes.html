Dial archetypes

Nineteen reference faces reduced to nine load-bearing structures. Every plate below strips out palette, texture and brand so that what remains is the arrangement of information — where time sits, where data sits, and what the eye reads first. We design SuperClock's own faces from these skeletons, not from the references.

Reality check — what our dial can actually hold

  • No sensors Heart rate, steps and calories appear on most references. We have none of them. app.fitness is a manual rep counter, not a wearable. Any subdial that borrows that layout needs a different payload.
  • What we do have Time, date, calendar events, weather & temperature, GitHub contributions, Claude usage, habits, todos, photos. Two shipped complications: date and temperature.
  • Viewing distance A wristwatch is read at 30 cm. This is a wall clock read across a room. Anything under roughly 40 px on the 1080 dial is decoration, not information.
  • Hardware Three Pi 4-class devices running for weeks. Per-frame work is heat. Two dials are round, one is square, one renders natively in LVGL and needs a second hand-built implementation.
  • Already covered Nine faces ship today — Minimalismo, Analog, Productivity, Square, Floral, Complications Light/Dark, World, Flip. Plates tagged adjacent overlap one of them.

Rim gauge

Structure
All data is banished to the perimeter as arc segments; the centre stays empty so the time reads instantly from across the room. Each arc is a range with a labelled start and end.

The one archetype that gets better at distance: arc length is readable when digits are not. Natural home for daylight remaining, calendar load, temperature range.

Almanac ring

Structure
Time widens outward: seconds and minutes at the centre, the month and the year at the edge. The dial answers "where am I in the year", not "what time is it".

Editorial composition

Structure

06

Outline typography

Structure
No indices, no ticks, no ornament — twelve oversized hollow numerals are the dial. Their counters and stroke gaps supply all the texture; everything else is a small quiet zone in the middle.
Distilled from
Night GlowOutlined numeralsWoven
Our angle
Cheapest to render and the most legible at distance of the nine. Type choice carries the whole face, which makes it a genuine identity piece rather than a layout exercise.

Generative field

Structure
A full-bleed algorithmic pattern is the entire dial. Two bare hands sit on top with no indices at all — the pattern's own drift is what tells you time has passed.

Orbital discs

Structure
Hands are removed entirely. Three concentric discs rotate and the time is read where each one meets a single fixed index at twelve. Motion is continuous rather than sweeping.

What I need from you

These are skeletons, not proposals. Pick the structures worth developing and I'll take each one into real design — palette, type, motion, and the specific SuperClock data it carries.

  1. Which plates do we develop? My read: 01 Chronograph, 03 Almanac and 06 Outline typography are the strongest — three genuinely different structures, none colliding with the existing nine, all readable across a room.
  2. How many faces are we shipping? Two done properly beats five half-built, and the slow device needs every shared face hand-ported to LVGL.
  3. Do these belong to one family or stand alone? A shared palette and hand geometry would make the set read as SuperClock rather than as nine unrelated downloads.
