What the research changed

Every plate on Sheet 01 — and every one of the nine faces we already ship — encodes time as angle. A hand, a pointer, a rotation. That is one family out of eight, and it happens to be the one the mood board sampled almost exclusively.

The other seven encode time as an aperture, a length, a count, a colour, a sentence, a cell in a lattice, or a form composed from parts that are not clock parts at all. Several of them get more legible at three metres, not less — which is the opposite of how subdials behave, and the single most useful thing this research produced.

B · Plates 10–18, drawn from the new families

Nine candidates that survive the three-metre test, run on a Pi 4 budget, and need only data we already have. Numbering continues from Sheet 01.

10

Regulator

Structure
Three units, three pivots, none concentric. Minutes get the whole dial on a central hand; hours shrink to a subdial above centre, seconds to one below. Precision is inverted — the unit you'd normally read first is the smallest.
Lineage
Bench régulateur, 18th c.Chronoswiss 1987Apple Exactograph
Our angle
A wall clock is glanced at for "roughly when", which is exactly what a regulator is bad at — unless we invert it and give the hours the full radius. That inversion is the design.
New groundFamily: angle
11

Aperture plate

Structure
No hands. A solid plate is cut with two windows; printed discs rotate behind it and step discretely on the hour and the minute. The dial's job is to hide, not to display.
Lineage
Pallweber 1883IWC 1885Cortébert 1920s
Our angle
The most distance-legible structure in the whole study — digits scale to a third of the dial. Distinct from Flip because nothing flips: the transition is a step, and the plate stays still.
New groundFamily: aperture
12

Daylight band

Structure
A 24-hour dial — one hand revolution per day, noon at the top — with the arc between sunrise and sunset filled bright. Arc length carries the season; the hand's position inside or outside it carries day versus night.
Lineage
Apple Solar DialSun ClockShepherd Gate 1852
Our angle
The band physically shrinks from June to December. A clock that shows you the year passing as well as the day, on hardware that lives in a room for years. Needs latitude and longitude — nothing else.
New groundFamily: length & area
13

Depletion disc

Structure
One disc, one wedge. The filled area is what's left of the day and it drains anticlockwise to nothing at midnight. No ticks, no numerals, no hands in the usual sense — the panel's own outline is the scale.
Lineage
Year progress barCalm technologyClepsydra
Our angle
The cheapest face we could possibly ship — one path, one update per minute — and the best structural fit for a circular panel we have found. Reads from any distance in the house.
New groundFamily: length & area
14

Count register

Structure
Four stacked registers of lamps — five-hour, one-hour, five-minute, one-minute — read by addition rather than by position. The eleven-cell minute row doubles as a coarse progress bar even if you never learn the code.
Lineage
Berlin Mengenlehreuhr 1975Binary / BCDFibonacci
Our angle
Huge high-contrast blocks, almost free to render, and the only archetype here that rewards learning it. The risk is real: a guest cannot read it at all.
New groundFamily: count
15

Sentence

Structure
A fixed field of words sits permanently on the dial at low contrast; the subset that spells the current time lights up. Reading is linguistic, not geometric, and resolution is deliberately five minutes.
Lineage
Qlocktwo 2009Fuzzy clocks
Our angle
Qlocktwo's 110-character matrix fails our distance test — 90px cells, well under the 120px floor. Ours has to drop to four or five lines of very large words, which makes it a typography commission rather than a grid.
New groundFamily: language
16

Colour sector

Structure
Twelve coloured sectors lie under an opaque disc cut with a single 30° wedge. The disc turns once per twelve hours, so exactly one hue shows at a time — the colour is the hour, the sliding edge of the cutout is the minute.
Lineage
Chromachron, Tian Harlan 1972The PresentAmbient Orb
Our angle
Reads from anywhere in the room, at any angle, because it is a field of colour rather than a mark. Harlan called it the anti-stress watch; on a wall it becomes room lighting that happens to tell the time.
New groundFamily: colour
17

Composed strokes

Structure
A grid of small analog dials whose hands stop being hands. Each pair aligns to draw one stroke of a seven-segment glyph, so twelve dials compose four digits — and between minutes the whole array choreographs, showing no time at all.
Lineage
ClockClock 24, Humans since 1982Flip-disc matrix
Our angle
Digits assembled at full canvas width, so they beat every other type treatment on distance — and the transit between minutes is the most watchable second of any face here. A round panel forces a 4×3 grid rather than 6×4.
New groundFamily: composed form
18

One hand, one day

Structure
One hand, one revolution per day — fifteen degrees an hour, a quarter degree a minute. The scale is graduated in quarter hours and precision below about five minutes is structurally unavailable, on purpose.
Lineage
Botta UNO 1986slow watches 2012Pre-1600 tower dials
Our angle
One heavy hand is the most legible object physically possible on this panel, and the design already accepts the two-or-three-minute ambiguity that distance imposes anyway. Fewest moving parts of anything here.
New groundFamily: angle
C · Findings that change our decisions

Three platforms agree on a lit-pixel budget. Our night mode doesn't meet it.

Wear OS quality rule WO‑P7 and Samsung's Watch Face Studio both cap always-on faces at 15% of pixels lit, averaged across the face and sampled every ten minutes for a whole day; Garmin is stricter at 10%, and shuts the screen off above it. Our night mode dims with CSS, which lowers brightness but leaves the same pixels lit. A face designed to a real pixel budget looks structurally different — thin strokes, no filled areas, and a small per-minute position shift for burn-in.

Source: developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/wear-app-quality · developer.samsung.com

No platform publishes a watch-face taxonomy. Only complication slots.

Apple names about 62 faces and groups them zero ways. Wear OS has no layout types. Pebble shipped watchfaces as a single flat bucket. Facer's 21 live categories are genres — Anime, Luxury, Space — with no Analog/Digital split at all. The only portable structural vocabulary anywhere is the slot system: corner, bezel, edge-arc, subdial, background, inline band. Our five categories are our own invention, which is fine — but we should stop treating them as an industry standard and name them for what our faces actually do.

Source: developer.apple.com/documentation/clockkit · developer.android.com/training/wearables/wff

Apple's gauge trichotomy is worth stealing outright.

Closed gauge, open gauge, segmented gauge — a ring completing 360° for a fraction of a whole; an arc with visible endpoints for an arbitrary min and max; a divided arc for values that change fast. That's a genuine information-design distinction we currently make by accident, and it maps directly onto Sheet 01's rim-gauge plate.

Source: developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/complications

Motion is a distance cue that geometry isn't.

The Mondaine station clock's second hand sweeps in 58.5 seconds then parks at twelve for a beat until the minute steps — you read the minute from the pause, not from a mark. Deadbeat seconds work the same way: sixty discrete six-degree positions read further away than any printed graduation. We already have useClockHands with a sweep mode, so this costs us nothing but a decision.

Source: Hilfiker/SBB 1944, red disc 1953 · Graham dead-beat escapement c.1675

Pebble is the only platform that published round-specific layout rules.

Keep information at least two pixels off the bezel. Avoid thin rings near the edge — mounting tolerance makes them visibly off-centre. Paginate rather than reflow, because every text row in a circle has a different available width. All three apply directly to a round panel in a 3D-printed housing, and the thin-ring warning is the one we are most likely to trip.

Source: developer.rebble.io/guides/design-and-interaction/in-the-round

Where this leaves the choice

Eighteen plates across two sheets. The useful cut is not "which look do we like" but which encoding families do we want represented — because we currently ship nine faces that all do the same thing.

One face from three different families beats three angle-faces: 13 Depletion disc (length/area — nearly free, best round fit, reads anywhere), 11 Aperture plate (aperture — the distance champion), and 12 Daylight band (length/area with real astronomical data, and it visibly changes across the year). If we want a fourth with genuine personality, 17 Composed strokes.
