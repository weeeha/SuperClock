# Neon faces: the twenty briefs

Draft faces for the round 1080 display, drawn in Canvas 2D by `faces/<id>.js` against `neon.js`. Gallery: `index.html` on the dev server (`/docs/drafts/neon-faces/index.html`). Nothing here is kiosk code yet; the picks get ported to real faces later, so every rule below is there to make that port cheap.

## Rules every face follows

- **Contract.** Read `neon.js` and `faces/tube.js` first. One file per face calling `NEON.face({ id, name, hue, accent?, accentFor?, idea, persist?, draw(ctx, T, N, state) })`. Draw in the 1000-unit square: the disc is radius 500 around (500, 500), and anything that tells the time stays inside r 470 (the bezel eats the rest).
- **Legible from a metre.** The panel is 12.7 cm across. Digits carrying the time are at least 150 units tall; hands at least 12 units wide; the time must read in one glance, before any decoration.
- **Colour.** Only `N.c`. One hue per face, plus at most one accent hue that marks exactly one quantity (named in `accentFor`). Brightness levels of the hue carry the hierarchy. White only as the tube core (N.glow does that).
- **Glow.** Only `N.glow` (strokes) and `N.glowDot` (points). Plain fills and strokes at low alpha are fine for unlit tubes, grids and glass. Never `shadowBlur`, `ctx.filter`, `getImageData`.
- **Time.** Only from `T`: `T.hourDeg`, `T.minuteDeg`, `T.secondDeg` (ticks), `T.sweepDeg` (smooth), `T.hh`, `T.mm`, `T.ss`, `T.h`, `T.m`, `T.s`, `T.sf`, `T.minuteFrac`, `T.hourFrac`, `T.dayFrac`, `T.date` (for the date only). Ambient motion uses `T.t` (seconds since load). Never `Date`, timers or `Math.random`; `N.hash(n)` gives stable randomness.
- **State.** Anything precomputed (star fields, lattices, particle seeds) lives on `state`, built once (`if (!state.ready) { ...; state.ready = true; }`).
- **Cost.** It must run on a Raspberry Pi 4 at 30 fps: the check reports canvas calls per frame; stay well under 3000. Batch many dim shapes into one path. `persist` (0.05 to 0.3) lets the gallery fade the last frame instead of clearing, for phosphor trails; use it only where the brief asks.
- **No invented content.** No text other than the time (and the date where the brief says). No labels, units, brand names or fake readings.

## The faces

1. **tube** (cyan, magenta for the seconds). Reference face, done. Hands and hour marks as cyan glass tube; a magenta spark runs the rim.
2. **sign** (pink). HH:MM as continuous bent-tube lettering, like a bar sign: each digit one tube path with rounded bends (arcs and lines, not seven segments), about 260 tall, one line across the middle. Where a real tube would jump between strokes, draw the blacked-out return tube dim (alpha about 0.12). Small dim standoff dots where the tube is mounted. The colon is two tube dots that breathe once a second (0.55 to 1). Once a minute one stroke stutters (two dips of about 150 ms), chosen by `N.hash(T.h * 60 + T.m)` so the same minute always does the same thing.
3. **nixie** (orange). Four nixie tubes in a 2 by 2 block, HH on top, MM below, each about 190 wide and 270 tall. Each tube: a rounded-top glass envelope outline (dim), a faint hex mesh, all ten cathode numerals stacked faintly behind (alpha 0.06), the lit numeral glowing orange with a white core. Numerals are thin stroked nixie shapes (oval 0, open 4). On a change the old numeral fades over about 120 ms while the new one ignites. Two small neon dots between the rows blink with the seconds.
4. **radar** (green, `persist` about 0.08). Range rings at r 150, 300, 450, crosshair and bearing ticks every 10 degrees, all dim. The sweep arm turns once a minute at `T.sweepDeg` (it is the second hand) with a fading wedge about 40 degrees behind it. Two contacts: the hour blip (large, r 230, at `T.hourDeg`) and the minute blip (smaller, r 400, at `T.minuteDeg`); each flares when the sweep passes and decays over about 2 s, computed from its angular distance behind the sweep, not from stored timers. A small HH:MM readout in monospace near the bottom inside the disc (about 60 tall).
5. **reactor** (blue). Segmented rings with small gaps, like an arc reactor: outer ring 60 thin segments (r 430 to 460) lit up to the current second, the newest brightest; middle ring 60 segments (r 360 to 405) lit up to the current minute; inner ring 12 thick segments (r 270 to 330) lit up to the current hour (12-hour). Unlit segments at alpha about 0.07. A core disc (r about 120) breathing slowly (4 s, 0.8 to 1) with HH:MM inside, about 90 tall. At :00 the seconds ring drains in 300 ms rather than snapping off.
6. **horizon** (magenta, cyan for the time). Synthwave: a magenta perspective grid on the lower half (horizon at y 560), lines converging on (500, 560) and scrolling toward the viewer, one spacing per second. A striped sun on the horizon whose height follows `T.dayFrac` (highest at noon, set below the horizon at night with only its glow showing). HH:MM in cyan seven-segment or tube digits above the horizon, about 170 tall. A dim magenta mountain line is optional.
7. **vector** (lime, `persist` about 0.15). A vector display: a dim graticule, HH:MM drawn as beam strokes (each digit one polyline in a simple vector font, about 180 tall). The beam re-traces the whole string every half second: the full path glows at a steady mid level and the stretch just behind the moving beam head is brighter. A small Lissajous figure near the bottom, frequency ratio from the hour and minute, phase turning with the seconds.
8. **plasma** (violet). A plasma globe: a glowing electrode (r about 60) at the centre and about eight thin tendrils writhing out to the glass at r about 480, each a jagged noisy polyline that wanders slowly. Two tendrils are the hands: the hour tendril (width about 10) reaches the glass exactly at `T.hourDeg`, the minute tendril (width about 7) at `T.minuteDeg`, and their contact points glow brightest. Ambient tendrils are thin and dim (intensity about 0.35) and keep away from the two hands. A faint glass rim.
9. **wordclock** (mint). A round word clock: a grid of capital letters filling the disc, rows trimmed to the circle, letters about 58 tall in a plain sans. Unlit letters at alpha about 0.1, the lit words glowing. English in five-minute steps: "IT IS TEN PAST TEN", "IT IS HALF PAST TEN", "IT IS QUARTER TO ELEVEN", "IT IS TEN O'CLOCK" (design the grid so every needed word exists in reading order; fill spare cells with stable letters). Four small dots near the bottom for the extra minutes 1 to 4. A changed phrase cross-fades over about 400 ms.
10. **tach** (red). A 270-degree tachometer, open at the bottom. The scale is the minutes 0 to 60, majors every 5 with numbers at 0, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60; the needle is the minute (smooth with the seconds); the redline band covers 55 to 60. The hour sits in the middle as a big gear-indicator digit pair, about 220 tall. A thin shift-light bar under it fills with the seconds. All red, the hierarchy by brightness.
11. **pulse** (green). A heart monitor: a trace on a line at y about 620, the write head (bright dot) at x about 760 moving the trace leftward; one P-QRS-T beat per second with the spike exactly on each second; the trace fades with age to the left. HH:MM above, about 180 tall, in tube or segment digits. A faint ECG grid at alpha about 0.05.
12. **constellation** (violet). About 180 tiny stars, stable positions, twinkling slowly. HH:MM drawn as star figures: each numeral a few bright star nodes on a 3 by 5 node grid joined by thin violet lines, about 220 tall. When a digit changes its lines draw in over about 800 ms. A satellite dot orbits at r 455 with the seconds.
13. **tunnel** (magenta). About ten concentric rings receding to a vanishing point near the centre, growing outward continuously so one ring leaves the rim each second; new rings start small and dim and brighten as they approach. Every ring carries two bright arcs: a wide one at `T.hourDeg` (about 26 degrees) and a narrow one at `T.minuteDeg` (about 18 degrees), so as the rings rush out they draw two streaks pointing at the hour and the minute. Slow and hypnotic, never strobing.
14. **outline** (yellow). Giant type: HH on the upper half, MM on the lower half, outline-only bold numerals about 360 tall. Set `ctx.font` (a heavy sans, for example `900 360px Inter, system-ui, sans-serif`) and pass `(c) => c.strokeText(...)` as the path to `N.glow`: each glow pass restrokes the text at its own width and alpha, so the outline glows like tube. The circle may crop the outer edges but every numeral stays readable. A small spark runs the rim at r about 480 for the seconds. Changed numerals cross-fade over about 300 ms.
15. **arcade** (cyan, yellow for the seconds). A cyan double-walled corridor around the rim (walls at r 405 and 470, a few stub walls). 60 small yellow pellets in the corridor (r 437). A yellow spark collector (a diamond with a short tail, not a chomping circle) travels clockwise at `T.sweepDeg`, collecting pellets: those behind it this minute are gone, those ahead remain, and all respawn with a flash at :00. HH:MM in the centre in a chunky pixel font (each font pixel a glowing square), cyan, about 160 tall.
16. **laser** (red). The hour and minute hands are laser beams crossing the whole disc: each runs from the rim through the centre to the opposite rim, bright on the pointing side and about 0.25 on the far side so direction reads. Hour beam wider than minute. Where a beam hits the rim, an impact flare with two to four short jittering sparks; a flare where the beams cross at the centre. About 40 dust motes drifting slowly that brighten when near a beam. Twelve dim dots on the rim for the hours. No seconds.
17. **spiro** (pink). A spirograph: a hypotrochoid whose R, r and d come from the hour, so each hour has its own figure, traced by a pen that completes one full figure per minute (drawn portion = `T.minuteFrac`), pen head bright, line thin (about 3 to 4). The finished figure fades over the first 3 s of the next minute. HH:MM in the centre in thin pink tube digits, about 150 tall, over the figure.
18. **hive** (lime). A flat-top hex grid (cell about 46) filling the disc, every cell outlined dim. HH:MM large in the centre (about 180 tall). The minutes are a hex progress ring: the cells whose centres sit between r 260 and 440, ordered by angle from 12 o'clock, first 60 of them, lit up to the current minute, the newest one pulsing.
19. **pendulum** (blue). A pendulum hanging from a pivot near the top (y about 110), rod about 520, bob radius about 40, crossing the bottom once each second (angle about 18 degrees times sin of pi times `T.sf`), the bob leaving a short fading light trail. HH:MM behind it, about 190 tall, in thin blue tube digits. A faint arc scale under the bob.
20. **dotmatrix** (mint). A round LED matrix: LEDs on a 26-unit pitch clipped to the disc, unlit ones faint (alpha about 0.07, one batched path), lit ones glowing. HH:MM in a chunky 5 by 7 font at two LEDs per font pixel, centred; under it the date (for example "THU 30 OCT" from `T.date`) scrolling right to left at one LED per font pixel. An outer ring of 60 LEDs at r about 470 lit up to the current second.
